Evasion attacks manipulate inference inputs to trick a deployed model into making a different prediction than intended, while poisoning attacks inject malicious or mislabeled data during training or fine-tuning to permanently alter the model's behavior.

**Key distinctions from the notes:**

*   **Timing:** Evasion happens at inference time (input manipulation), whereas poisoning occurs during training/fine-tuning (data injection).

*   **Target Property:** Evasion targets integrity of the prediction for a given input. Poisoning targets the integrity and knowledge substrate of the model.

Model Answer (6.38s):
The 7-step framework for game economy design is:

1. **Define Purpose**: Clarify why the economy exists before adding numbers, considering pacing, choice, or retention.
2. **Identify Core Resources**: Categorize into core progression (XP, gold), optional upgrades, and special/event-based currencies.
3. **Map Sources and Sinks**: Ensure every resource has a source (faucet) like loot or quests and a sink (drain) like crafting or repairs to prevent inflation or starvation.
4. **Set Pricing & Progression Curves**: Design early-game generosity with late-game earning; test real time-to-reward.
5. **Prototype and Simulate**: Use spreadsheets to model fake play sessions before coding.
6. **Test with Real Players**: Run closed playtests to observe actual behavior like hoarding or exploiting.
7. **Tune, Track, and Maintain**: Monitor telemetry post-launch and make small, measured adjustments rather than sweeping overhauls.

This approach treats the economy as a living system that requires constant tracking and communication with players to maintain trust.
